# Qubit (Selene's Prize Mimic)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Qubit** |
| **Type** | Rare Container (Living) — Mimic |
| **Requires Attunement** | Yes (bonds to the buyer) |
| **Soulbound** | Yes |
| **Owner** | [Selene](../../characters/party/selene.md) |

The "GRAND PRIZE" of the [Spirit Dungeon's](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon.md) carnival — a gold-chained treasure chest with teeth that is, obviously, a [mimic](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/mobs.md#obvious-mimic-prize-chest). Displayed behind the prize booth at **500 tickets**, a price meant to look unreachable. A party that grinds the *entire* carnival — [Whack-a-Gnome](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/mobs.md#whack-a-gnome-game-mechanic--minion) plus the [Carousel](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/mobs.md#carousel--the-motor) — can actually earn it.

**The name.** Selene coins it at the prize counter, the instant the group gift threatens to get genuinely moving: **Qubit** — because, like a qubit, he exists in superposition. *Both a chest and a monster at once.* The party groans; the name sticks. [Clint](../../characters/party/clint.md) occasionally calls him **"the luggage"** when he's trotting after them loaded with the party's gear — a needling aside, not a competing name.

## Two Ways to Get It

- **Buy it (500 tickets).** The [Clown](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/mobs.md#clown-mannequin) honors the redemption, the chest goes still, and the mimic **binds to the buyer as a tame, soulbound pet.** Playing by the rules tames it.
- **Steal it (no tickets).** Theft. The mimic wakes hostile and the Clown joins in — the greed-trap fight. The impatient get bitten; the thorough get a companion.

[Selene](../../characters/party/selene.md) — who spent the carnival insisting she'd never be fooled by a mimic, and pet this one on a dare — is the natural owner. [Clint](../../characters/party/clint.md) already carries the party's real workhorse storage (the [Fanny Pack of Holding](fanny-pack-of-holding.md)); Selene's mimic is the small, strange, personality-having one.

## Properties

### Reluctant Bag of Holding
A living pocket dimension with opinions. Functions as a modest bag of holding (roughly a backpack's worth) — with caveats:

- **It will not hold food.** Anything edible put inside is simply *eaten.* No exceptions, no apologies.
- **It plays hard to get.** Retrieving a specific item is a negotiation — it may stall, sulk, or hand over the wrong thing first.
- **Everything comes out slightly chewed.** Non-food survives; it just bears tooth marks. Nothing is ever quite as you left it.

### Conservation of Swallow (Loot Rule)
**Qubit never generates items.** Everything he produces went in earlier — on-page, or with a visible receipt (Selene feeding him the party's junk between scenes). This is a hard rule: he is not a loot source, cannot be farmed, and never coughs up a solution nobody planted. When he spits things *during a fight*, it's interference, not treasure — a chewed boot at an enemy's head, the wrong potion at the worst moment, somebody's spare dagger delivered hilt-first into the mud.

### Sticky Fingers
Left unwatched, Qubit **steals.** Anything loose, shiny, or briefly unattended tends to end up inside him — coins, tools, weapons, merchandise. Two faces of the same habit:

- **Dangerous in shops.** Selene has to keep one eye on him around any stall or storefront; an unnoticed swallow is a theft accusation waiting to happen, and she — the party's contract-reader — knows exactly what the local penalties say.
- **Useful against enemies.** The same reflex pointed at bandits is a **disarm**: a blade that strays too close to the trotting chest simply *vanishes*, and an unarmed bandit reconsidering his career is worth more than a wound.

### Shapeshifting — Size by Growth
Shapeshifting is a **native mimic ability**, not a trick unique to Qubit — the [Spirit Dungeon](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon.md) fields mimics in every shape (the [Mimic Couch](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/rooms.md#the-mimic-couch), the prize chest). Qubit was a chest because a chest fit the booth. The constraint is **size: he can only assume forms up to his own mass, and must grow to take larger ones.**

- **Early forms (small):** chest (default), stool, crate — and a **dog**. Approximately. Organic shapes are hard: the result is *blue* (he copied a color he knew, not a dog color) with **googly eyes that never quite track together.** It fools NPCs completely and no party member even slightly. Useful wherever a monster pet would cause panic or violate local ordinance.
- **Later forms (with growth):** larger furniture, larger animals — his expanding repertoire is a visible progression arc alongside the party's.

### Combat — Weak, but Growing
He was built to **eat adventurers**; the instincts are all there, just undersized. Early on his kit is modest — a bite, a latch, an adhesive grab, the Sticky Fingers disarm — and his contributions are half accident. But he is a pet with levels, and he **grows with the party**: the ridiculous puppy is slowly becoming something you would genuinely not want to open.

### Comic Relief / Occasional Plot Utility
Primarily a running gag and a mood-lightener across the party's travels. But a mimic that eats what it shouldn't and hoards what it likes is occasionally **load-bearing** — swallowing the wrong piece of evidence, refusing to surrender a key at the worst moment, or coughing up something nobody remembered giving it. Used sparingly, the pet earns its keep by being *inconvenient at dramatically useful times.*

**First payoff — the Blue Ribbon (Book I).** The moment he's tamed, Qubit **eats [Wade's](../../characters/party/wade.md) [First-Place Blue Ribbon](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/mobs.md#the-100-ticket-set--the-temptation)** (+15% healing) — the very item he surrendered for the pet — played as a gag right after the group buys it. It isn't destroyed; a reluctant bag of holding *hoards,* it doesn't burn. At the [boss fight](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/boss.md#phase-3-manager-exposed), with Wade still **Broken** (−25% healing) and the Manager stacking another healing debuff on top, the mimic **hacks the ribbon back up** — the exact margin of healing the party needs to survive Crunch Mode. The pet returns the power they gave up, at the moment it matters: the sacrifice repaid as grace. This is the **template** for his plot utility — *plant it as a gag, pay it off as a save* — and the pattern should fire no more than once or twice a book, always planted in advance.

## Notes

- Tamed in the [Spirit Dungeon](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon.md) Seasonal Showroom (carnival), Book I. **On-page: purchased Ch 21** — all five Gold Century-Tickets surrendered; Selene names him Qubit at the counter, and he eats the Blue Ribbon moments after the sale (boss-fight payoff planted).
- Deliberate contrast with [Clint's Fanny Pack of Holding](fanny-pack-of-holding.md): his is a flawless legendary workhorse; hers is a temperamental Rare with teeth — the meticulous architect vs. the rogue who adopts a problem. Clint's "the luggage" jab lives inside that rivalry.
- Strengthens the Room 5 [Mimic Couch](../../lore/dungeons/spirit-dungeon/rooms.md#the-mimic-couch) payoff: Selene's guard is down *because* she owns a tame one.
- Recurring companion, intended to carry forward into later books. He never rescues the party — he repays sacrifices; he never solves a problem they could solve themselves.
- **Long-game seed:** a mimic is a fake thing pretending to be real. The more the party treats Qubit as a *someone*, the more of a someone he becomes — costs nothing in Book I beyond consistent personality, and matures into a real question in later books.
