# Tablet of Annihilation

The in-sim macguffin driving the [World Quest](world-quest.md). A physical manifestation of the simulation's reset-threat, cast in fantasy form.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Artifact (unique, indestructible to normal means) |
| **Origin** | Transformation of the iQuantum quantum computer at the Transition |
| **Appearance** | Black stone tablet with shifting binary runes |
| **Location** | Ancient Red Dragon's hoard (Saratoga ruins of iQuantum HQ) |
| **Function** | Ends existence if not destroyed within 10 years of the Transition |

## Appearance in the Narrative

Appeared in Chapter 13 at the moment of the Transition. [Clint](../characters/party/clint.md) was at the quantum computer terminal when the "Simulation corruption detected. Reset?" prompt appeared. The computer then transformed into the Tablet. Clint watched it happen before the Ancient Red Dragon arrived and destroyed the building.

The Tablet has been in the dragon's hoard at the Saratoga ruins from that moment forward. It is visible at a distance to anyone who approaches the lair and is referenced as the World Quest's physical target for five books.

## Properties

- **Indestructible by normal means.** Conventional weapons, magic, environmental damage — none affect it. Even legendary-tier attacks fail. The sim treats it as an immovable object.
- **Functionally inert until the timer runs out.** The Tablet doesn't *do* anything during the 10-year window except exist as a visible threat. It is the ticking clock rendered as matter.
- **Cannot be moved by non-dragon-tier forces.** Its placement is the dragon's responsibility; it resists relocation.

## Destruction

The Tablet can only be destroyed by something that exceeds its own immovability — an unstoppable force. In the series' design, that force is [Katsuragi](../items/weapons/katsuragi.md), Clint's Unique-tier console-crafted katana, using its Cancel-Reset ability.

**Mutual annihilation.** When Katsuragi cuts the Tablet, both artifacts are destroyed. Unstoppable force meets immovable object; neither survives. This is by design — the sim allows root-level outcomes only when the console-cheat tools that could enable further exploitation are themselves sacrificed.

Clint gains root access upon the cut. See [`lore/world-quest.md`](world-quest.md) and `memory/project_series_endgame.md` for the full endgame sequence.

## Meta-Origin

*(Author-only until the Book 5 reveal.)*

The Tablet is the in-sim rendering of the simulation's reset-prompt. When the quantum computer displayed "Simulation corruption detected. Reset?" during the Transition, real-Clint outside the sim cancelled the reset. The system's fear-rendering engine converted the cancelled threat into a quest artifact. The Tablet *is* the cancel-reset prompt, translated into matter by the AI template engine.

Destroying it at the finale cancels the threat permanently. Katsuragi's Cancel-Reset ability is not a coincidence of naming — it's the only function that can write the actual cancellation into the sim's root layer.

See `meta/real-world.md` for the full real-world origin of the World Quest.
