# Proficiencies

The third progression axis, alongside [class level](classes-and-professions.md) and [profession rank](classes-and-professions.md#profession-rank). Where a **class** defines *what* you can do and a **profession** defines *what you can make*, a **proficiency** defines *how well you do it* — the earned technique behind every swing, cast, and step.

> **Terminology:** "Proficiency" is the use-based mastery axis described here. Do not confuse it with **Skill**, which is an *ability type* on stat blocks (`Type: Skill`, as opposed to Spell / Song / Smite). An ability may be a Skill; how well you execute it is governed by the relevant Proficiency.

## What Proficiencies Cover

Broad, use-based masteries — defined as the story needs them rather than exhaustively catalogued. Typical categories:

- **Weapon proficiencies** — Sword, Dagger, Shield, Polearm, Bow, Unarmed, and so on.
- **Magic proficiencies** — by school: Fire, Frost, Illusion, System, Temporal, Healing, etc.
- **Utility proficiencies** — Stealth, Perception, Persuasion, Riding, and the like.

A character accrues proficiencies naturally through use. There is no cap on how many you hold, but — as with professions — advancing one takes focused effort, so people specialize.

## Proficiency Rank

Proficiencies use the **same 1-99 scale and named tiers as [profession rank](classes-and-professions.md#profession-rank)** — one consistent progression grammar across the whole system (class level, profession rank, and proficiency rank all run 1-99).

| Level | Rank | Meaning |
|-------|------|---------|
| 1-9 | Novice | Fumbling the basics. Power without control. |
| 10-24 | Apprentice | Competent fundamentals. Reliable under calm conditions. |
| 25-49 | Journeyman | Independent skill. Holds up in a real fight. |
| 50-74 | Expert | Recognized skill. Reads openings, wastes little. |
| 75-89 | Master | Top of the field. Technique becomes instinct. |
| 90-98 | Grandmaster | Extraordinary. Does things others think impossible. |
| 99 | **System-Granted Title** | A unique, personal title — as with professions: one holder per proficiency, contested by a system-adjudicated challenge. |

## How Proficiencies Advance

Proficiency advances through **genuine use**, not rote repetition — the same philosophy as [profession rank](classes-and-professions.md#profession-rank) ("genuine mastery, not repetitive grinding"). Unlike class [XP](xp.md), it is not strictly danger-gated: you *can* drill a proficiency upward in safety — but rote practice plateaus early, and **real stakes accelerate it.** A blade swung in a life-or-death fight teaches more in one exchange than an hour against a straw dummy. Pressure sharpens technique; boredom stalls it.

## Attribute vs. Proficiency vs. Class

Three different things resolve every action:

- **Attribute** — raw capacity. STR is how *hard* you can swing; INT is how much power you can push into a spell.
- **Proficiency** — technique. Sword proficiency is how *well* you place that swing — accuracy, finesse, exploiting openings, economy of motion. Fire proficiency is control and efficiency, not raw wattage.
- **Class ability** — the move itself (Smite, Fireball), gated by [class level](classes-and-professions.md).

A high-STR / low-proficiency fighter hits like a truck and misses like an amateur — enormous damage when it lands, wild and wasteful when it doesn't. A high-proficiency / normal-stat veteran lands everything, punishes every opening, and can out-*technique* someone far stronger. **Power is not mastery.**

## The Gap: Hacked Stats, Novice Hands

This is the axis the [main party](../characters/roster.md) never cheated — and it defines them.

Before the Transition they console-boosted their attributes, HP, and mana to the ceiling, and even faked a profession ([Clint's](../characters/party/clint.md) Grandmaster [Enchanting](../professions/enchanting.md)). Proficiencies were **just as hackable.** They simply never got to them — a rushed, survival-first hack job, the same imperfection that left [Clint's own Luck at 4](../characters/party/clint.md). The window closed with the Transition, and the console — the quantum computer, now the Tablet of Annihilation — is gone.

The result: **god-tier stats, Novice proficiencies.** They hit like trucks and miss like amateurs.

- [Clint](../characters/party/clint.md) can bend a blade with STR 20 and swing it like a man who took one weekend fencing class.
- [Selene's](../characters/party/selene.md) DEX makes her a blur, but low Blade proficiency is why her first kills ([Ch 11](../story/chapter-summaries.md)) were clumsy and traumatic, not clean.
- [Vanessa](../characters/party/vanessa.md) can pour enormous mana into a Fireball; low Fire proficiency is why it goes everywhere — including over her own party.

Post-Transition, **every point of growth is earned** — class levels *and* proficiencies. Proficiency is the purest earned axis: the one number the party cannot cheat, only grind out of real fights. Their arc is the cheaters slowly becoming genuinely, deservedly skilled — and until then, a disciplined veteran with ordinary stats can still school them.

Clint's hacked Grandmaster Enchanting is the tell: a faked rank that grants real capability on a hollow foundation ("the skill, if not the materials"). His combat proficiencies show what the whole party looks like *without* the hack — Novice.

## Notes

- Proficiencies are deliberately **framework, not catalogue** — specific proficiencies enter canon as the story needs them (a rank-up notification at a dramatic beat, a technique unlocking mid-fight). The category list above is illustrative, not exhaustive.
- Skill-ups make good prose beats precisely *because* they're earned: a proficiency tick lands hardest right after a hard-won, genuinely dangerous success — the moment the cheaters earn something for real.
