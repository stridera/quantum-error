Rebekah Sullivan is a recruiter who joined the group through mutual friendships rather than through iQuantum. After the Transition she becomes a Temporal Bard — the party's buffer, morale engine, and source of hope when things look dark.

## Appearance

- **Height / build:** 5'8", a comfortable, sturdy-soft build; an arresting, warm presence.
- **Skin:** Fair, with freckles across her face — a touch of whimsy.
- **Hair:** Fiery red, worn loose and very long — it falls nearly to her waist ("nearly to the table" when she's seated, Ch 1).
- **Eyes:** Bright green; an open, expressive face.
- **Voice:** A soft Scottish lilt, echoing her ancestral heritage.
- **Attire:** practical layered travel clothes; [Mirror Shard](../../items/accessories/mirror-shards.md) pendant.
- **Illustration hook:** her hands — fingers mid-pluck on faintly shimmering vertical strings of light, a harp made of the moment itself (see [Playing Style](#playing-style)).

### Epic Reflection (Mirror Room, Ch 18)

Her future self wore **a beautiful dress** — deep green shot through with light-thread, glowing faintly like her strings — **and carried a guitar that looked half-medieval and half-futuristic**: a carved, lute-like body and scrollwork on one half, luminous fretwork with strings of hard light on the other. The instrument bridges the guitar she lost at the Transition and the temporal strings she plays now. Everything in the glass had a deep power pulsing through it.

## Personality & Party Role

The social fabric of the group — warm, loud, hopeful; the friend who pulls everyone to the table. In combat she's the force multiplier: her songs keep the party moving forward and grant significant buffs. When things look darkest, Rebekah is the reason they keep walking.

## Playing Style

Rebekah does not carry an instrument. [Chrono Resonance](../../classes/temporal-bard.md) lets her play *the temporal strings themselves* — she plucks at invisible threads of time while she sings, fingers moving as if on a harp no one else can see. The music comes from both her voice and the strings she's playing; the strings vibrate at frequencies most people can't hear but everyone can feel. When she's deep in a song, onlookers describe it as watching a harpist play an instrument made of the moment itself.
